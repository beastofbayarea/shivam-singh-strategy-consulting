# Stopping a rewards launch that made every new customer less valuable

The direct-to-consumer plan had >$50 million of launch momentum and allocated 70% of spend to social acquisition. Base lifetime value was only 0.8× CAC.

During my June–December 2023 Rakuten experience, I led the strategy decision across Product, Finance, Partnerships, Operations, Analytics, enterprise clients, employees, and executives.

The question was not “how fast can we launch?” It was “should we scale a relationship that loses value with every acquired user?”

## I put the downside distribution in front of executives

I ran 10,000 scenarios across media auction cost, conversion, churn, transaction frequency, gross margin, and customer value. Media spikes used a long-tailed distribution, churn stayed bounded, and LTV remained a range rather than management’s preferred point.

In 68% of scenarios, the direct model lost >$20 million in year one.

That was a modeled risk distribution—not an observed loss or 68% certainty of failure. It showed that plausible single-variable improvement did not repair structural economics. I recommended stopping the funded launch.

## The replacement changed distribution, not creative

Employers and existing enterprise relationships could introduce the marketplace inside trusted relationships. The B2B2C contract included:

- minimum guarantee;
- MSA addendum where an existing agreement allowed it;
- standardized onboarding;
- activation/transaction reporting;
- renewal owner;
- support boundary.

Two early routes de-risked execution. An employee launch produced 14,500 active users in 30 days without an external MSA. Existing travel clients used addenda, reducing the contract path from a ~9-month planning benchmark to 11 weeks.

No partner could contribute >15% of new users, preventing paid-media dependence from becoming employer concentration.

## Scale evidence preceded logos

Customers active on five separate days/month were 60% more likely to retain. I used the association for onboarding and partner selection, not causal proof.

Raw identities stayed regional. Deterministic hashed matching connected exposure, activation, transaction, and retention. Early pilots were discounted 30–40% in planning because the first employer received extraordinary support.

Partner cohorts were compared with geographically matched direct control. Google’s [geo-experiment research](https://research.google/pubs/measuring-ad-effectiveness-using-geo-experiments/) supplies the randomized benchmark; this project used matching, so residual selection remained.

## Operating result

| Unit economics | Direct baseline/control → scale gate → B2B2C result | Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| CAC | ~$42 → below LTV → $8.50 | Attributable partner/onboarding cost / activated customer; ~80% lower |
| Day-30 retention | 34% → beat discounted control → 67% | Day-0 activated cohort active Day 30; +33 points |
| Transactions | 1.8/user-month → repeat utility → 3.2 | Completed transactions / active customer-month; +77.8% |
| LTV:CAC | 0.8× → >1× plus uncertainty margin → 4.5× | Discounted contribution / CAC |
| Payback | 18 months → <12 → 4 months | Cumulative contribution recovers CAC |
| Contract cycle | ~9 months benchmark → accelerate via existing agreement → 11 weeks | First commercial step to executed addendum |
| Concentration | no guardrail → ≤15%/partner → 15 partners in 6 months | New-user share and executed partner count |

These are operating-team outcomes from the pivot. I owned the fact base, simulation, stop recommendation, distribution model, pilot design, measurement, concentration gates, and executive alignment. Product/Partnerships/Operations owned contracts, launch, service, and P&L.

The strategic decision was to stop growth in order to preserve value. Better ads could not fix negative unit economics; a reversible enterprise route changed acquisition, retention, contracting, privacy, and concentration together.
